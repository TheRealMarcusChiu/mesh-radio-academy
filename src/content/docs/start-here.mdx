Mesh Radio Academy teaches two community mesh radio systems, **Meshtastic**
and **MeshCore**, side by side and without favoring either. Every required
lesson and lab is designed to work in your browser without a radio. Hardware
exercises will be optional extras.

## What is ready today

This is the foundation of the site. You can:

- [send a simulated message](../) and follow it through each layer,
- read the [planned course outline](../curriculum/),
- mark pages as read, which is stored only in this browser.

The numbered lessons are **not written yet**. Each one is published only
after its technical claims are checked against primary sources and the
lesson is reviewed. Nothing on this site is a finished lesson until it
says so.

## Four layers to keep apart

The message on the [home page](../) passes through four layers. The course
keeps returning to them, because most confusion about mesh radios comes from
mixing them up.

| Layer | In plain words | Example on the home page |
|---|---|---|
| App | What you see and type | Writing and reading the message |
| Phone–radio link | How your phone talks to your own radio | A short Bluetooth Low Energy link |
| Mesh packet | The envelope mesh radios read to decide what to do | Radio A wraps the message; radio B opens it |
| Radio signal | The physical transmission through the air | The hop from radio A to radio B |

Two radios can share the same radio settings and still be unable to exchange
messages if they speak different mesh protocols. The module on radio versus
protocol will show exactly why.

## How the course labels what it tells you

- **Teaching model:** a simplified picture made to explain an idea, such as
  the home page trace. Useful, but not a description of exact firmware
  behavior.
- **Design assumption:** a value chosen for an example, like a transmit
  power in a calculation.
- **Documented or measured fact:** something backed by a cited source or a
  recorded test, with the version and date it applies to.

## Planned learning paths

These paths will open as lessons are published.

1. **No hardware:** every lesson and lab in the browser, using simulations
   and sample data.
2. **Two radios:** the same lessons plus optional exercises with two of your
   own nodes, after checking the rules where you live.
3. **Operator:** planning, testing and maintaining a small neighborhood mesh
   with your local community.
