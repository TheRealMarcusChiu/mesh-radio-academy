Everyone near you shares one channel. Every relay, beacon, ACK and retry takes a slice of airtime that nobody else
can use. This module counts those slices and then shows how to measure delivery without fooling yourself. There
are **no field measurements** here: every number is either a formula result with stated inputs or a synthetic
example.

## Goals and prerequisites

You need the airtime formula from [Module 01](../01-rf-foundations/) and the forwarding rules from
[Module 04](../04-meshtastic-under-the-hood/) and [Module 06](../06-meshcore-under-the-hood/). Run the diamond in
the [routing playground](../../labs/routing-playground/) and check airtime in the
[RF sandbox](../../labs/rf-sandbox/) as you go.

## Identical assumptions, stated once

To compare fairly, everything that isn't the protocol stays the same:

- **RF:** the course's teaching profile (SF7, 125 kHz, CR 4/5, 8-symbol preamble, explicit header, CRC on). A
  frame with an assumed 40 physical bytes lasts **82.176 ms**. <Cite ids="CUR-C21 RF-C01" /> Real frames differ in
  size between protocols and packet types; the shared 40-byte size is an assumption that isolates *counting*.
- **Traffic:** one channel message from A that every node should see.
- **No loss, no collisions,** a fixed relay order. Real relay delay is randomized. <Cite ids="MT-C06" />
- **Meshtastic:** every node `CLIENT`, hop limit as stated. A client relays when hop limit remains and it hasn't
  heard a duplicate before its turn. <Cite ids="MT-C03 MT-C08" />
- **MeshCore:** nodes marked R are dedicated repeaters; the rest are companions with repeating off. Each repeater
  forwards its first copy of a flood once. <Cite ids="MC-004 MC-010" />

What differs is labeled as **protocol behavior**, not as one being "better".

- A–B, B–C, C–D and B–E are the only radio links.
- C and E can't hear each other, so neither can cancel a relay on hearing the other.
- In the MeshCore version, B and C are dedicated repeaters; A, D and E are companions.

The **chain** is A–B–C–D in a line (B and C are repeaters on MeshCore). The **diamond** is Module 04's, with B and C
able to hear each other (B and C are repeaters on MeshCore).

| Topology | Meshtastic transmissions | MeshCore transmissions | Everyone received? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chain, hop limit 3 | **4**: A, B, C, and D (D receives with one hop left and relays to nobody new) | **3**: A, B, C | Yes, both |
| Chain, hop limit 2 | **3**: A, B, C | 3 (MeshCore has no per-packet hop limit here) | Yes, both |
| Diamond, hop limit 1 | **2**: A, B; C cancels on hearing B <Cite ids="MT-C03" /> | **3**: A, B, C; both repeaters forward their first copy | Yes, both |
| Sparse, hop limit 3 | **5**: A, B, C, E, D (C and E are hidden from each other; D still has budget) | **3**: A, B, C; companion E stays quiet | Yes, both |

Airtime at 82.176 ms per frame:

| Topology | Meshtastic | MeshCore |
|---|---|---|
| Chain (hop limit 3) | 328.704 ms | 246.528 ms |
| Diamond | 164.352 ms | 246.528 ms |
| Sparse | 410.880 ms | 246.528 ms |

**Read this carefully.** Neither column wins everywhere. Meshtastic's every-client-can-relay design reaches E-like
corners with no planning and cancels copies when relays hear each other; it spends extra transmissions at the
edges and where relays are hidden. MeshCore's fixed repeaters send a predictable number of copies, but only where
someone deployed a repeater: put D one hop past a companion and it gets nothing. These counts are about **one
message under ideal conditions**, not real capacity.

## Where else the airtime goes

| Source | Meshtastic | MeshCore |
|---|---|---|
| Hop budget | Each extra hop of budget lets copies travel one relay farther; edge nodes relay into silence. <Cite ids="MT-C08" /> | Flood scope and hop limits on repeaters bound floods. <Cite ids="MC-005" /> |
| Backoff | Randomized, SNR-weighted relay delay; `ROUTER` uses an earlier window and skips ordinary cancellation. <Cite ids="MT-C05 MT-C06" /> | Not modeled in the course. |
| Identity beacons | Node info, schema default every 900 s. <Cite ids="CUR-C09" /> | Repeater flood adverts, default every 47 h. <Cite ids="CUR-C17" /> |
| Position | Schema default 15 min, only when position changed significantly. <Cite ids="CUR-C09" /> | Optional location inside adverts. |
| ACKs | Every DM ACK is another relayed packet. | PATH+ACK floods back the first time, then goes direct. |
| Retries | Up to three attempts per DM from an isolated sender. <Cite ids="MT-C12" /> | App-driven; firmware has no universal retry rule. <Cite ids="MC-013" /> |

**Worked example: background traffic.** Assume 20 Meshtastic nodes in the sparse pattern above, each sending node
info 4 times an hour and a changed position up to 4 times an hour, each original relayed into 5 transmissions,
40 bytes each. That's 8 × 5 = 40 frames per node per hour, 40 × 82.176 ms ≈ 3.29 s per node, ≈ 65.7 s across 20
nodes: about **1.8 %** of every hour spent on beacons before anyone types a message. Every input is an assumption;
change any one and the result moves in proportion. Halving the beacons halves it.

## Measuring delivery honestly

**Packet delivery ratio (PDR)** = unique messages delivered ÷ messages scheduled, always written with the sample
size. <Cite ids="CUR-C22" />

A synthetic run: A schedules 20 numbered messages to D, one every 30 s, with fixed RF settings and fixed endpoints.

| Direction | Scheduled | Unique delivered | PDR | On-air attempts (separate) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A → D | 20 | 17 | **85 % (17/20)** | 31: originals, relays and retries counted at A, B and C |
| D → A | 20 | 14 | **70 % (14/20)** | 36 |

What you can and can't say:

- At n = 20, one message moves PDR by 5 percentage points, so 85 % versus 70 % is a difference of three messages.
  That's a hint of asymmetry worth repeating, not a proven property of the link. <Cite ids="CUR-C22" />
- **Attempts are not in the denominator.** 31 attempts for 17 deliveries describe airtime cost, not delivery.
- **"Delivered" means the recipient displayed it**, not that the sender saw an ACK. A missing ACK can hide a
  delivered message. <Cite ids="MC-030 MT-C13" />
- Write measured counts, RSSI and SNR separately from what you infer (route, cause). <Cite ids="CUR-C23 RF-C12" />

## Hidden nodes, less traffic, better placement

- **Hidden nodes.** Two transmitters that can't hear each other can overlap at a receiver between them, which then
  decodes neither. The course simulators don't model this collision. <Cite ids="RF-C13" /> In the sparse example,
  C and E are such a pair for anything around B.
- **Less background traffic** means fewer chances for overlaps and more free airtime. You can show it changed the
  beacon count; you can't show it fixed delivery unless delivery is measured before and after under the same
  conditions.
- **Better antenna placement** can raise received signal and margin. A before/after test with one change can
  support "in this run, PDR went from 14/20 to 18/20 after raising the antenna". It can't support "height adds
  20 %" without many repeats across days, because weather, people and other traffic change too. <Cite ids="CUR-C23" />

## Your turn: change one thing

1. In the Meshtastic chain, what hop limit is the smallest that still reaches D, and how much airtime does it save
   against hop limit 3?
2. In the MeshCore sparse neighborhood, E wants to talk to D. What has to change, and what does one channel message
   then cost?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Hop limit 2: B and C relay, D receives with none left. 3 transmissions, 246.528 ms, saving one frame
   (82.176 ms) per message. <Cite ids="MT-C08" />
2. Nothing new is needed for E's flood to reach D: E's message goes to repeater B, B repeats to C, C repeats to D.
   E (companion), B and C transmit: 3 frames, 246.528 ms. The constraint is the other way round: a companion that
   *only* E could hear would never forward. Check eligibility before blaming RF. <Cite ids="MC-004" />
</details>

## Knowledge check

<details>
<summary>1. Why does the Meshtastic diamond need fewer transmissions than the MeshCore diamond in the table?</summary>

C is a `CLIENT` that hears B's copy before its turn and cancels. MeshCore repeaters forward their first copy of a
flood. Same RF, different protocol behavior. <Cite ids="MT-C03 MC-010" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>2. A report says "PDR 90 %". What's missing?</summary>

The sample size (9/10 and 900/1000 mean very different things), the direction, the fixed conditions, and a
separate count of on-air attempts. <Cite ids="CUR-C22" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>3. Is raising the hop limit to 7 a cheap way to improve reliability?</summary>

No. It lets every message travel farther, so edge and distant nodes spend airtime repeating copies nobody needs,
for everyone on the channel. Use the smallest limit that reaches your destinations. <Cite ids="MT-C08" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>4. Your sender showed 3 failures, but the recipient saw all 20 messages. What was the PDR?</summary>

20/20. Delivery is what the recipient displayed; the missing ACKs are a return-path or ACK problem to investigate
separately. <Cite ids="MC-030 MT-C13" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>Deeper dive: why counts aren't capacity</summary>

- Capacity also depends on collisions, capture, duty-cycle limits and queue behavior, none of which the counts
  include. The course labs report these as omitted effects.
- Meshtastic's late-relay roles and favorite-router hop rules change counts in infrastructure-heavy meshes. <Cite ids="MT-C05" />
- Header, encryption and path bytes add airtime on top of your text; frame size grows with path length on MeshCore. <Cite ids="RF-C05" />
</details>
