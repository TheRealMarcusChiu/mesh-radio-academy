MeshCore runs on the same kind of radio as Meshtastic but is a different protocol: the two don't exchange native
messages, even on identical RF settings. <Cite ids="LW-C02" /> This lesson repeats Module 03's checkpoint
sequence on MeshCore, so you can compare the two step by step. As before, nothing here was run on real hardware for
the course, every step has a simulated equivalent, and app menu names are described rather than invented.

## Goals and what you need

- **Finish first:** [Module 02](../02-hardware-first-boot/) (board, antenna, recovery) and
  [Module 03](../03-first-meshtastic-link/) (checkpoints, delivery indicators).
- **With hardware:** two identical boards that have MeshCore 1.18.0 companion targets, band antennas, USB data
  cables, and a phone each. A third identical board for the repeater exercise. <Cite ids="HW-C04" />
- **Without hardware:** a notebook and the [routing playground](../../labs/routing-playground/) (MeshCore side),
  which starts from two companions and two dedicated repeaters.

The phone talks to the companion over BLE using the companion protocol. That's an app protocol, not the radio
packet format. <Cite ids="MC-018 LW-C03" />

## The checkpoint sequence, MeshCore edition

1. **Confirm hardware and band.** Same as Module 03: label, revision, 902–928 MHz in the US.
2. **Attach the matching antenna before power.** <Cite ids="HW-C09" />
3. **Back up supported settings, privately, and know what erase does.** Reflashing from Meshtastic to MeshCore
   doesn't carry over identities or keys. The MeshCore flasher warns that erasing destroys the device identity:
   your contacts would know a "new" node afterwards. <Cite ids="HW-C12" /> Use only the procedure your board's
   manufacturer documents; on the T1000-E, Seeed's exact-image instructions override generic advice. <Cite ids="HW-C06" />
4. **Choose the exact firmware image and role.** The companion BLE image for your board at 1.18.0. Companion,
   repeater and room server are separate images. <Cite ids="HW-C04" />
5. **Verify the installed version** on the device or in the app's device information, and write it down.
6. **Set region and RF parameters.** Both companions need the same frequency, bandwidth, spreading factor and
   coding rate. There's no universal value: in North Texas the community's join page published 910.525 MHz,
   62.5 kHz, SF7 and "CR5" (checked 2026-11-09), while its map page sends MeshCore users to Discord for current
   settings. Check your own community's maintained page. <Cite ids="NTX-C02" />
7. **Connect the client.** Pair the app over BLE. If pairing fails after a reflash, remove the old Bluetooth
   pairing and try again. <Cite ids="HW-S08" />
8. **Send locally:** advertise, add the contact, send a DM (next section).
9. **Observe the recipient** on Phone D.
10. **Save a sanitized test record.** No private keys, full public keys or locations.

## Adverts, contacts and the first direct message

MeshCore nodes announce themselves with **adverts**: a signed packet carrying the node's public key, name, role
and optional location.

- A **zero-hop advert** reaches only radios that hear you directly. Repeaters don't pass it on.
- A **flooded advert** can be relayed by eligible repeaters, so farther nodes can learn you. <Cite ids="MC-006" />
- Hearing an advert gives you an **identity** for a contact. It doesn't give you a route: a new contact starts with
  an unknown path. <Cite ids="MC-007" />

For a DM, both companions need each other as contacts, from adverts or added manually. A signed advert proves the
sender holds that key, not that the name is who you think. Compare the public key with your partner in person
before you trust it. <Cite ids="MC-019" />

Then send a short DM from A to D. With no known route, A **floods** it. D replies with a returned path bundled
with an ACK, and A stores that path for next time. <Cite ids="MC-008" /> On a two-node link the "path" is empty:
D heard A directly.

Read the evidence like Module 03, with MeshCore's four separate events: A's companion accepted the send; a matching
protocol ACK came back; D's app displayed it; D's human replied. <Cite ids="MC-030" />

## Channels: public and private

- The **public channel** uses a published key. Anyone with that key can read and post. Encryption on a known-key
  channel doesn't make it private. <Cite ids="MC-020" />
- A **private channel** limits readers and writers to people holding its key. Every key holder can still type any
  sender name; the name in a group message isn't verified. <Cite ids="MC-021" />

Post one short line on a private test channel from A and look for it on D. If you post on a real public channel,
keep it to a brief introduction (Module 10 covers first contact with a community).

## Third node: a dedicated repeater

Now make A and D unable to hear each other. Use distance or walls, never a missing antenna or detuned settings.
Then work through three stages, changing one thing each time.

1. **No relay.** A's DM floods; nobody forwards it; no ACK. A sees "not confirmed", which on its own doesn't
   tell you why.
2. **Middle node as a companion.** Put a third companion B between them. Companions have repeating off by default,
   so B hears A and stays quiet. Same result. <Cite ids="MC-004" />
3. **Middle node as a repeater.** Flash B with the **repeater** image for that board. Set its radio to the same
   values as A and D: the documented console default `869.618,62.5,8,5` is outside the US band, and the setting
   needs a reboot. <Cite ids="CUR-C16" /> **Change its admin password** from the shipped `password` before
   deploying, and don't paste the command's reply anywhere: it echoes the new password. <Cite ids="MC-024 CUR-C20" />
   Now A's flooded DM is relayed by B, D replies, and A stores the path `[B]`. The next DM goes **direct**
   through B.

A repeater also sends flooded adverts on its own, every 47 hours by default, which other nodes use to learn it. <Cite ids="CUR-C17" />
Module 06 follows these packets byte by byte.

## When a room server helps

A **room server** is a third role, also its own image. Clients log in; the server **decrypts and stores** posts
and hands them to members who connect later. <Cite ids="MC-026" />

| | Channel | Room server |
|---|---|---|
| Who keeps history | Only each listener's app, for what it heard | The server, in a bounded queue (32 posts by default, build-configurable) <Cite ids="MC-027" /> |
| Someone was offline | They miss it | They can catch up on recent posts |
| Who can read content | Every key holder | Logged-in members **and the server and its operator** |
| Good for | Live chat among people who are on air | A notice board for a group that's on air at different times |

A room server is a trusted endpoint, not opaque storage, and the course promises no retention time.

## Knowledge check

<details>
<summary>1. D heard A's zero-hop advert. Does A now have a route to D?</summary>

No. An advert gives an identity. A's path to D starts unknown and is learned from D's returned path after a DM. <Cite ids="MC-007 MC-008" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>2. You put a spare companion in the middle and nothing changed. Why?</summary>

Companions have repeating off by default. Use a dedicated repeater image, or deliberately change the companion's
setting with the community's agreement. <Cite ids="MC-004" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>3. A message on your private channel says it's from "Sam". How sure can you be?</summary>

Only that a key holder sent it. Group sender names are unverified text. <Cite ids="MC-021" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>4. Your group wants late arrivals to see what was said. Channel or room server, and what's the trade?</summary>

A room server, which stores recent posts. The trade: the server and its operator can read everything, and history
is a bounded queue, not an archive. <Cite ids="MC-026 MC-027" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>5. You reflash a working companion and its contacts now show a "new" node. What happened?</summary>

The flash erased the device identity, so it has a new key pair. Erase steps destroy identity; back up first and
expect to re-exchange contacts. <Cite ids="HW-C12" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>Deeper dive: the companion protocol versus the radio packet</summary>

- The app sends commands such as "send this text to contact X" over BLE using the companion protocol. The companion
  firmware turns that into an over-air packet with its own header, path and encrypted body. Capturing BLE traffic
  tells you nothing about the radio frame layout, and the reverse. <Cite ids="MC-018" />
- Path entries can be 1, 2 or 3 bytes wide; nodes older than 1.14 drop multibyte paths. Keep a mixed-version mesh
  on one-byte paths until every forwarder is updated. <Cite ids="MC-014 MC-015" />
- The release notes introduce a remote companion CLI permission with 1.18.0 and app 1.50.0. Leave it off for a
  first link. <Cite ids="MC-025" />
</details>
