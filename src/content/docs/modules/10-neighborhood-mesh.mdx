A neighborhood mesh is shared infrastructure: it uses spectrum your neighbors also use, it may sit on someone
else's building, and it needs looking after for years. This module is about planning one responsibly. It doesn't
send you up a ladder: no hardware is needed, everything here can be done on paper or in the
[field planner](../../labs/field-planner/), and any physical installation should follow permission, qualified advice
and local code.

## Goals and prerequisites

You need link margin and Fresnel clearance from [Module 01](../01-rf-foundations/), airtime counting from
[Module 07](../07-reliability-capacity/) and the privacy checklist from [Module 08](../08-privacy-security-rules/).

| Role | Meshtastic | MeshCore |
|---|---|---|
| Personal handheld or home node | `CLIENT` (relays as needed) or `CLIENT_MUTE` (never relays) <Cite ids="MT-C07" /> | Companion, repeating off by default <Cite ids="MC-004" /> |
| Coordinated fixed relay | `ROUTER` or `ROUTER_LATE` only where the community agrees: they relay early or late and skip ordinary duplicate cancellation <Cite ids="MT-C05" /> | Dedicated repeater image |
| Shared history for a group | (No direct equivalent in the course scope) | Room server, with its operator inside the trust boundary |

**Placement is engineering, not a slogan.** "Put all relays on the same roof" fails for a reason you've already
counted: two relays that hear exactly the same nodes add copies without adding coverage (Module 07's diamond). Put a
relay where it hears nodes that otherwise can't hear each other, and no more relays than the links need.

## Antennas: polarization and height

- **Polarization:** keep antennas at both ends in the same orientation, normally vertical. A mismatch adds loss
  that simple formulas ignore. <Cite ids="RF-C14" />
- **Height:** what matters is clearing the first Fresnel zone, not only seeing the other antenna. On a 2 km link at
  915 MHz the zone is about 12.8 m in radius at the midpoint. A relay a few meters higher can clear roofs and trees
  that sit inside that zone. <Cite ids="RF-C11" />
- **Gain:** a higher-gain antenna narrows the pattern; it may point energy over your neighbors' heads. Check
  antenna changes against the device's authorization (Module 08).

## Power and maintenance budget

Battery runtime is a calculation with assumptions, not a product property. <Cite ids="RF-C08" />

```text
Average current  = f_tx·I_tx + f_rx·I_rx + f_sleep·I_sleep   (all at the same supply rail)
Runtime (hours)  = capacity_mAh × usable_fraction × efficiency / average current
```

**Worked synthetic example:** 1 % transmit at 120 mA, 98 % receive at 15 mA, 1 % sleep at 0.1 mA →
**15.901 mA** average. With 2000 mAh, 80 % usable and 90 % assumed conversion efficiency: 2000 × 0.8 × 0.9 / 15.901 ≈
**90.6 hours**. <Cite ids="CUR-C26" /> Every input is invented. Real nodes add screens, LEDs, GPS, Bluetooth,
regulator losses and other people's relay traffic; a relay listens almost all the time, so its receive current
dominates. Manufacturer current figures are measured under stated conditions, not whole-node averages. <Cite ids="HW-S03" />

Battery limits that come from manufacturers, not from the formula:

- The T1000-E may only be charged between 0 and 45 °C: operating in the cold is not the same as charging in it. <Cite ids="HW-C05" />
- The RAK19007's solar input must not exceed 5.5 V, and battery polarity must match the board's drawing. <Cite ids="HW-C09" />

A **maintenance plan** for each infrastructure node:

| Task | How often | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Check firmware advisories, plan updates | Monthly | Both projects' first-party pages |
| Read battery voltage or solar charge log | Monthly, and after cold spells | Compare with the budget |
| Inspect enclosure, seals, cable entry | Twice a year, never in storms | IP ratings don't cover degraded seals |
| Confirm admin password changed and ACL current | After any membership change | <Cite ids="MC-024" /> |
| Review beacon and advert intervals | When the mesh grows | MeshCore repeater flood adverts default to every 47 h <Cite ids="CUR-C17" /> |
| Record who has physical and admin access | Always current | Kept private |

## Weatherproofing, grounding and safe installation

An outdoor node adds **conductive paths**: the antenna feed line, USB or DC power, solar wiring and any mains supply.
Protection has to account for every one of them; no single surge arrestor makes an installation safe. <Cite ids="HW-C10" />

- Use current local electrical and building requirements and a qualified installer for anything mounted outdoors.
  The ARRL material the course cites is educational background, not code. <Cite ids="HW-S12" />
- Don't install, inspect or test outdoors during thunderstorms. <Cite ids="HW-S13" />
- A manufacturer's IP65 rating is not a promise against immersion, condensation or a cable you added. <Cite ids="HW-C05" />
- Get the property owner's **permission** before placing anything, and their agreement on access for maintenance.
- As a beginner, stay off roofs and masts and away from overhead lines. A window or an indoor high shelf is a
  legitimate first site.

## Change plan: community first

Before adding a relay or traffic, check what your area already uses, **by area and by protocol**.

1. **Find the maintained source.** For the dated North Texas example (checked 2026-11-09), NTX Mesh's join page
   says Meshtastic guidance depends on county, with `LONG_FAST` as its default and a county preset map. <Cite ids="NTX-C01" />
   For MeshCore the join page listed 910.525 MHz, 62.5 kHz, SF7, "CR5", while the map page points to Discord for
   current settings. <Cite ids="NTX-C02" /> Those are a community's dated settings, not regulation and not
   universal. Outside North Texas, find your own community's page.
2. **Notice where sources disagree.** The NTX broadcast-interval table and its prose don't match; ask the maintainers
   rather than picking one. <Cite ids="NTX-C03" />
3. **Don't read a map as a census.** The NTX map is a preset map, not live coverage, and no map shows every node. <Cite ids="NTX-C04 CUR-C25" />
4. **Coordinate infrastructure before deploying it.** Tell the community where, which protocol, which role, which
   settings and who maintains it.
5. **First contact, low traffic.** Listen first, send one short introduction, wait for a human reply, and take
   repeated tests out of band. <Cite ids="NTX-C05" />
6. **Remember the spectrum is shared.** Unlicensed operation has no protected frequency and must not cause harmful
   interference. <Cite ids="REG-C01" />

## Your turn: write a change plan

Your street wants a relay so three homes can reach a park two blocks away. A neighbor offers a second-floor window
facing the park. Write a one-page change plan with: protocol and role, settings source and date, why this site
(what it hears that the homes don't), power budget assumptions, maintenance owner, permission, and the first-contact
message you'd send.

<details>
<summary>A model answer (Meshtastic version)</summary>

- **Protocol and role:** Meshtastic, because the community page for this area lists it; the relay as `ROUTER_LATE`
  only if the community agrees, otherwise `CLIENT`. Homes stay `CLIENT`.
- **Settings:** preset and slot from the community's county guidance, recorded with the date checked.
- **Why this site:** the window can see the park and all three homes; the homes can't see the park. That's coverage
  a home node can't provide.
- **Power:** USB from the neighbor's mains supply; no battery outdoors; indoors, so no outdoor conductive paths.
- **Maintenance:** one named person checks advisories monthly and visits by appointment.
- **Permission:** the neighbor's written OK, including how to remove the node.
- **First contact:** "New relay planned near [approximate area], Meshtastic, community preset. Can anyone near the
  park confirm they hear the test node this weekend?" Then wait.
</details>

## Knowledge check

<details>
<summary>1. Why can adding a second relay next to the first make a mesh worse?</summary>

If both hear the same nodes, they add copies of every packet without reaching anyone new: more airtime, more chances
of collision, no gain. Module 07 counted exactly this: each MeshCore repeater forwards its first copy of a flood,
and Meshtastic `ROUTER` roles skip ordinary cancellation. <Cite ids="MC-010 MT-C05" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>2. The community map shows no nodes in your area. Can you assume nobody's there?</summary>

No. Maps only show what reported to them. Listen first and ask the community. <Cite ids="CUR-C25 NTX-C04" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>3. A solar node's battery runs flat every winter although the summer budget said 5 days. Name two assumptions to revisit.</summary>

Any two of: lower usable capacity in the cold, no charging below the charge temperature limit, less solar input,
more relay traffic than assumed, extra loads such as a screen. <Cite ids="RF-C08 HW-C05" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>4. Is a coax surge arrestor enough to protect a rooftop node?</summary>

No. Every conductive path (feed line, power, solar wiring) needs a plan, done to local code by a qualified person. <Cite ids="HW-C10" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>Deeper dive: two Fresnel-zone sanity checks</summary>

- Midpoint radius grows with √distance: a 4 km link has a midpoint radius about √2 times the 2 km one, roughly 18 m.
- Near either end, the zone narrows, so obstacles close to an antenna matter less than the same obstacle halfway.
  This is geometry from the ITU formula, not a coverage prediction. <Cite ids="RF-C11" />
</details>
