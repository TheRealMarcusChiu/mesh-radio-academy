You don't need hardware to finish this course. This module is for the moment you consider buying it. Its
rule is simple: **check the evidence before you buy, and check the antenna before you power on.** Nothing
here has been physically tested for this course; every row says what kind of evidence backs it.

## Check before you choose

For any board, confirm all of these from the manufacturer and the firmware project, not from a shop listing:

1. **Band and regional variant.** The radio and the antenna must both match your region's band; in the US,
   902–928 MHz. Some boards come in separate high-band and low-band versions.
2. **Exact model and revision.** Revisions can differ in power circuits and pins. Write down what the label says.
3. **Firmware target at the course baseline.** A build target must exist for that board in the exact
   firmware version you plan to run. <Cite ids="HW-C11 HW-C04" />
4. **Roles you need.** Can it run as an ordinary client, or as a dedicated repeater?
5. **Client support.** Which app connects to it, over which link (BLE, USB, Wi-Fi), in which versions.
6. **Power.** Input voltage, battery chemistry, connector type and polarity.
7. **Antenna.** Is a band-correct antenna included? Where does it connect?

## Kits: two radios, three radios, or none

**Two-node starter.** Two identical RAK4631(H) + RAK19007 assemblies, each with matching band antennas, a
USB **data** cable and an ordinary 5 V USB supply. Batteries and non-conductive cases are optional. Run both
as Meshtastic `CLIENT`, then reflash both as MeshCore BLE companions for a second run. Identical hardware
means differences you see come from the software, not the boards. <Cite ids="HW-C07" />

**Three-node relay.** Add a third identical assembly. On Meshtastic, all three can be `CLIENT`; the middle
one forwards. The `REPEATER` role is deprecated, so the course doesn't use it. <Cite ids="HW-C08" /> On MeshCore,
use two companions plus one dedicated `RAK_4631_repeater` image: companion and repeater are different builds.
A line on paper doesn't force a real route, so the end nodes must genuinely be unable to hear each other.
Don't fake that by detuning or removing antennas.

**No hardware.** Use the simulated send in [Module 00](../00-start-here/) and the
[routing playground](../../labs/routing-playground/). The playground's two-protocol diamond covers what the
three-node kit teaches about relaying, and it is labelled as a simulation, never as captured traffic.

These kits are **documentation-checked candidates**. Before you call one "known good", record the actual PCB
revisions, bootloaders, firmware files and app versions you used.

## Rules before radio

Owning a radio does not settle whether a particular firmware, power setting and antenna are allowed. In the
US, unlicensed devices operate under Part 15: you get no protected frequency and must not cause harmful
interference. <Cite ids="REG-C01" /> Swapping antennas can change what is authorized, so matching connectors
are not enough. <Cite ids="REG-C02" />

Before transmitting, find the FCC ID on the device or module label and look it up in the FCC Equipment
Authorization database, noting frequency range, power and antenna conditions. This course has **not** done
that for any board above. Outside the US, start with your own national regulator. A later module covers the
rules in depth.

## Safe first power-up

These steps don't erase anything. Do them in order.

1. **Attach the correct band antenna before applying power.** RAK warns explicitly against powering the
   transceiver without its antennas. <Cite ids="HW-C09" />
2. **Use a USB data cable and an ordinary 5 V supply.** Charge-only cables can't show a serial port or
   drive. Seeed advises a normal USB supply rather than a fast charger for the T1000-E. <Cite ids="HW-S07" />
3. **If you fit a battery, check the board's polarity drawing,** not wire colors or whether the plug fits.
4. **Write down the label**: model, revision, band and any FCC ID. Keep it private if it identifies you.
5. **Don't flash yet.** First back up whatever settings the current firmware lets you export, and keep
   that backup out of public places. Exports can contain keys.
6. **Find, but don't use, your board's recovery entry** (table above) so you know it before you need it.
7. **Change default passwords** on any dedicated MeshCore repeater or room server before deployment;
   the build files ship with `password`. <Cite ids="MC-024" />

## The first-link checkpoint sequence

Modules 03 (Meshtastic) and 05 (MeshCore) will follow this same order step by step:

1. Confirm the hardware model and band.
2. Attach the matching antenna.
3. Back up supported settings privately.
4. Choose the exact firmware file for your board target and role.
5. Verify the installed version on the device.
6. Set the region and RF parameters.
7. Connect a compatible client.
8. Send one short local message.
9. Observe it on the recipient.
10. Save a sanitized test record (no keys, no precise locations).

## Recovery decision tree

Work from the first symptom that matches. **Erase steps are marked. They destroy settings and identity
keys, so back up first.** <Cite ids="HW-C12" />

<details>
<summary>The device powers on but the computer sees no serial port or drive</summary>

1. Swap to a known **data** USB cable and another port; check the supply.
2. Close other apps that may hold the port.
3. Enter the board's bootloader with the manufacturer's procedure: Heltec PRG/RST, RAK double-tap, T1000-E
   button plus cable sequence. On RAK and T1000-E a USB drive should appear; check its info file.
4. Only now consider reflashing with the exact image for your board.
   **⚠ Erase warning:** "full erase" or "factory reset" options destroy settings and keys. Don't treat erase as
   a first diagnostic. The MeshCore flasher itself warns that erase destroys the device identity. <Cite ids="HW-C12" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>The firmware boots but without the role you expected</summary>

1. Check the installed **version and build target** on the device itself, not only what the phone app shows.
2. Meshtastic roles are settings on one image; check the role setting. MeshCore companion, repeater and room
   server are **different images**: a companion image can't become a dedicated repeater by changing a
   setting. <Cite ids="HW-C04 HW-C11" />
3. Reflash the correct image only after backing up.
</details>

<details>
<summary>The phone won't pair after flashing</summary>

1. Forget or remove the device in the phone's Bluetooth settings, then pair again. An old pairing can
   linger after reflashing, and Seeed's MeshCore guide calls for re-pairing. <Cite ids="HW-S08" />
2. Confirm your app version is documented for the firmware version ([baselines](../../reference/baselines/)).
3. Try USB if your board and app support it, to separate a BLE problem from a firmware problem.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Two nodes still can't exchange messages</summary>

Check from the bottom layer up, one change at a time:

1. Same **protocol** on both? Meshtastic and MeshCore don't exchange native messages. <Cite ids="LW-C02" />
2. Same **region, frequency and modem settings**? <Cite ids="MT-C15" />
3. Same **channel and key**, or for a direct message, does each side know the other as a contact?
4. Does each node's **role** allow what you expect?

Modules 03 and 05 turn each of these into a controlled mismatch drill.
</details>

## Task: choose a kit on paper

A learner wants to compare Meshtastic and MeshCore fairly, with the fewest variables. Using only the table,
which two-node kit would you pick, and which three things must they check at purchase that the table can't
confirm?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Two identical RAK4631(H) + RAK19007 assemblies: same chips on both tracks, BLE and USB on both, and a
dedicated repeater target if they later add a third node. At purchase they must confirm: (1) the module is
the H band variant with US915 antennas, (2) the actual PCB revision and that it is not the RAK4631-R
bootloader variant (or plan its conversion), and (3) what antennas and cables the kit really includes.
Neither the FCC grant conditions nor any app pairing is confirmed by this course.
</details>

## Knowledge check

<details>
<summary>1. You plug in a new Heltec board with no antenna "just to see the screen". What is the risk, and what should you do first?</summary>

If the firmware transmits, an unterminated transmitter wastes power and can be damaged. Attach the correct
band antenna before powering any LoRa radio. <Cite ids="HW-C09" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>2. A shop lists a "T1000" tracker. Can you use the T1000-E instructions and image?</summary>

Not without confirming it is exactly a **T1000-E**. T1000-A, -B and -S are different products, and the
manufacturer says to use only the exact image. <Cite ids="HW-C05 HW-C06" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>3. Your MeshCore companion node won't forward other people's packets. Is that a fault?</summary>

Not necessarily. Companions have repeating off by default; dedicated repeater images are built for
forwarding. Check the image and its settings before assuming a fault. <Cite ids="MC-004" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>Engineering detail: what a "build target" does and doesn't prove</summary>

- A target is an entry in the firmware's build configuration (`platformio.ini`). At Meshtastic `54e0d8d` the
  three targets are marked actively supported at support level 1; the T1000-E entry sets
  `MESHTASTIC_EXCLUDE_WIFI=1`. <Cite ids="HW-S14" />
- At MeshCore `81dbae5`, each board has separate environments for companion (BLE, USB, sometimes Wi-Fi),
  repeater and room server. <Cite ids="HW-S10" />
- A target proves someone configured a build for a board family. It doesn't prove every silent hardware
  revision, retail bundle, supplied antenna or app combination works.
- Heltec's V3.2 PDF cover says V3.2 while its revision history and footer disagree. The course keeps that
  inconsistency visible rather than guessing. <Cite ids="HW-C01" />
</details>
