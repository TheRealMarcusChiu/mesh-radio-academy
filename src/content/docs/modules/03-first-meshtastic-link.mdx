This is the first lesson where a real radio could transmit. You can do all of it **without hardware**: every step
has a simulated equivalent, and the drills work as paper predictions. If you do have two radios, this lesson is
your script. Nothing here was run on physical hardware for this course; where a step depends on an app screen,
the lesson names the setting rather than inventing a menu path.

## Goals and what you need

By the end you will have one documented, repeatable link between two Meshtastic nodes, or a simulated one, and
you will have broken it four different ways on purpose.

- **Finish first:** [Module 00](../00-start-here/) (layers), [Module 01](../01-rf-foundations/) (RF settings and
  airtime) and [Module 02](../02-hardware-first-boot/) (board choice, antenna, recovery).
- **With hardware:** two identical boards with a Meshtastic 2.7.26 target, matching band antennas, USB data
  cables, a phone or computer per node. <Cite ids="HW-C11" />
- **Without hardware:** a notebook. Every checkpoint below has a written simulated equivalent, and the drills
  are prediction exercises with revealed answers. The [packet inspector and mismatch bench](../../labs/packet-inspector/)
  is the click-through version of the drills; the [routing playground](../../labs/routing-playground/) shows the
  same message moving once relays join in Module 04.

1. Phone A talks to Node A over Bluetooth Low Energy or USB. That link carries an app protocol, not radio packets.
2. Node A transmits over LoRa. Node B receives only if region, modem settings and frequency slot match.
3. Node B decrypts with the channel key (or, for a direct message, its private key) and hands the text to Phone B.
4. Both nodes run the ordinary `CLIENT` role.

## The checkpoint sequence, Meshtastic edition

This is the same ten-step order Module 02 introduced. Do the steps for **both** nodes before moving on. Each
step says what to check, why, and the no-hardware equivalent.

1. **Confirm hardware and band.** Read the board label: model, revision, band. In the US the radio and antenna
   must both be for 902–928 MHz. *Simulated:* write the label of an imaginary RAK4631(H) in your record.
2. **Attach the matching antenna before power.** A transmitter without its antenna can be damaged. <Cite ids="HW-C09" />
3. **Back up supported settings, privately.** Exported configuration can include channel keys. A channel-sharing
   URL encodes every enabled channel's settings, key included, so treat it like a password. <Cite ids="CUR-C12 MT-S23" />
4. **Choose the exact firmware target and role.** Pick firmware 2.7.26 and your board's target
   (`heltec-v3`, `rak4631` or `tracker-t1000-e`) explicitly; don't accept whatever "latest" the flasher offers. <Cite ids="HW-C11 MT-S08" />
   Use the `CLIENT` role: an ordinary endpoint that can still relay. `ROUTER` and `ROUTER_LATE` are infrastructure
   roles with different relay timing and duplicate handling, meant for coordinated fixed sites, and `REPEATER`
   is deprecated. <Cite ids="HW-C08 MT-C05" /> If you don't want your node to relay at all, `CLIENT_MUTE`
   does that. <Cite ids="MT-C07" />
5. **Verify the installed version on the device.** Check the firmware version the node reports, not just what
   you meant to flash. Matching version prefixes between app and firmware don't prove they work together. <Cite ids="MT-C25" />
6. **Set region, then RF parameters.** A node with region `UNSET` does not transmit at all; region comes
   first. <Cite ids="CUR-C01" /> An unset modem preset means `LONG_FAST` and the hop limit defaults to 3
   <Cite ids="CUR-C02" />, but your local community may use a different preset. In North Texas, for example, the
   community's guidance depends on county. <Cite ids="NTX-C01" /> Both nodes need the same region, the same preset
   and the same frequency slot. <Cite ids="MT-C15" />
7. **Connect a compatible client.** Pair the phone app over BLE or connect over USB. The Android 2.8.3 and Apple
   2.7.27 apps were identified as current for this course, not tested with this firmware. The Python CLI 2.7.11
   is the documented technical client ([operator appendix](../../appendix/operator-tools/)). <Cite ids="MT-C24 MT-C25" />
8. **Send locally.** First one short message on your private test channel, then a direct message (DM) to the
   other node. A DM needs the recipient's public key, which nodes learn from each other's node-info broadcasts;
   if it's unknown, the send fails instead of quietly downgrading to weaker encryption. <Cite ids="MT-C19" />
9. **Observe the recipient.** Look at Node B's screen or Phone B. What the *sender's* app shows is a different
   piece of evidence (next section).
10. **Save a sanitized test record.** Time, firmware, boards, region, preset, slot, roles, what was sent, what was
    seen where. No keys, channel URLs or precise locations.

Hold on to the record from step 10. Every drill below starts from it, and Module 09 builds on it.

## Delivery indicators are not read receipts

The sender's app reflects four different events. Only the last one involves a person.

| What happened | What it proves | What it doesn't prove |
|---|---|---|
| The node accepted the message from the app | It's queued on your node | Anything was transmitted or heard |
| **Implicit ACK**: your node heard someone rebroadcast it | Some relay repeated your packet | That the destination decoded it, or a human read it <Cite ids="MT-C13" /> |
| **Explicit ACK** from the destination | A protocol reply came back from that node | That a person read it; in 2.7 it is not a signed receipt <Cite ids="MT-C14" /> |
| The other person replies | A human saw something | That every earlier message arrived |

On a two-node link with no relays there is nobody to rebroadcast, so a DM's success indicator depends on the
explicit ACK. Without one, the sender retries: an isolated sender schedules the first send plus two
retransmissions. <Cite ids="MT-C12" />

## Worked example: a sanitized first-link record

This is a **synthetic** record written the way you would write a real one. No radio produced it.

```text
Test 03-01  (SYNTHETIC example record, not a capture)
Firmware    2.7.26.54e0d8d on both nodes (checked on device)
Boards      Node A: RAK4631(H)+RAK19007, label rev recorded privately
            Node B: same model
Region      US      Preset LONG_FAST      Slot  explicit, same on both (number kept in private notes)
Roles       both CLIENT                   Hop limit 3
Channel     private test channel, 32-byte key shared in person (key NOT recorded here)
Client      Android app over BLE (version written from About screen)
Placement   two rooms apart, indoors, antennas vertical
Sent        A→channel "test 1"   B shows it: yes
            A→B DM  "test 2"     B shows it: yes   A shows explicit ACK: yes
Notes       no relays present, so no implicit ACK expected
```

Every line is something you can check again later. Notice what's missing: the channel key, the channel URL, any
coordinates. "Two rooms apart" is enough to repeat the test.

## Four mismatch drills

Each drill changes **one** setting on Node B, from the baseline above. Predict first, then check (or reveal the
expected result). Restore the baseline before the next drill.

- Drills 1 (preset) and 2 (frequency slot) fail at the RF channel: Node B never decodes a frame.
- Drill 3 (channel key) passes RF but fails decryption: the frame arrives and is unreadable to B.
- Drill 4 (unavailable destination) fails at the reply: nothing comes back, so the sender never gets an ACK.

</FlowDiagram>

<details>
<summary>Drill 1: modem preset. Node B switches from LONG_FAST to a different preset.</summary>

**Layer:** RF. **Expect:** B shows nothing from A and A gets no ACK. The two nodes now use different spreading
factor or bandwidth, so B can't demodulate A's frames even on an overlapping frequency. The preset is shared by
every channel on the node, so changing channels won't fix it. <Cite ids="MT-C15" />
**Restore:** set B back to the recorded preset.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Drill 2: frequency slot. Node B's slot is set to a different number.</summary>

**Layer:** RF. **Expect:** the same symptom as drill 1, for a different reason: the two radios are on different
center frequencies. A sneakier version: with slot 0 the slot comes from a hash of the **primary** channel's name,
so renaming the primary channel on one node can move it to another frequency even though a secondary channel
still matches perfectly. <Cite ids="CUR-C03 MT-C16" />
**Restore:** set the recorded slot. Note in your record whether you use slot 0 or an explicit one.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Drill 3: channel key. Node B keeps the channel name but gets a different key.</summary>

**Layer:** decryption. Keep the slot explicit for this drill so that only the key changes. **Expect:** the frames
reach B over the air, but B can't decrypt channel messages, so nothing appears in B's channel. A node in rebroadcast
mode `ALL` could still relay those frames without reading them. <Cite ids="MT-C07 MT-C15" /> DMs use public-key
encryption rather than the channel key, so they may still get through: a good illustration that "the channel" and
"a DM" are separate paths. <Cite ids="MT-C19" />
**Restore:** give B the original key **in person or over a private channel**, never by posting the URL.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Drill 4: unavailable destination. Node B is switched off; A sends a DM.</summary>

**Layer:** delivery. **Expect:** A accepts and transmits the message, hears no rebroadcast and no explicit ACK,
retries (first send plus two retransmissions in this firmware), then reports failure. <Cite ids="MT-C12" />
Nothing in A's view says *why*: off, out of range and wrong key all look the same from the sender. That is why
the recipient's observation (checkpoint 9) matters.
**Restore:** power B on and repeat the baseline send once.
</details>

## Restore the baseline without publishing a key

After the drills, return both nodes to the recorded baseline. Do it **from your private notes, not from a
screenshot of a QR code or a pasted channel URL.** The URL carries the key; anyone who sees it can read and write
your channel. <Cite ids="CUR-C12" />

1. Re-enter region, preset and slot from the record.
2. Re-enter the channel name. Re-share the key in person, or generate a fresh 16- or 32-byte key and share that
   privately. <Cite ids="CUR-C13" />
3. If a channel URL or QR code ever appeared in a photo, chat or repository, treat that key as public and replace it.
4. Repeat checkpoint 8 once and log it as the restored baseline.

## Your turn: name the drill from the evidence

Four synthetic observation sets are below. For each, name the mismatch and the layer, then say what single check
would confirm it.

| Case | Node A's app | Node B | Extra clue |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | DM: no ACK after retries | Nothing at all | B's slot field reads differently from A's |
| Q | Channel text: queued, no ACK expected | Nothing in the channel | A DM from A *does* appear on B |
| R | DM: no ACK after retries | Screen is dark | |
| S | Channel and DM both fail | Nothing at all | B's primary channel was renamed yesterday; slot is 0 on both |

<details>
<summary>Suggested answers</summary>

- **P:** frequency slot (drill 2), RF layer. Confirm by comparing the slot setting on both nodes.
- **Q:** channel key (drill 3), decryption layer. The DM works because it uses public-key encryption, not the
  channel key. Confirm by comparing the keys privately, never by posting them.
- **R:** unavailable destination (drill 4). Confirm by powering B and watching its screen during the next send.
- **S:** slot 0 derives the frequency from the primary channel's name, so renaming it on B moved B to a different
  frequency: an RF problem caused by a channel change. Confirm by comparing primary channel names, or set an
  explicit slot on both. <Cite ids="CUR-C03" />
</details>

## Knowledge check

<details>
<summary>1. Your new node is powered, paired and configured with the right preset, but nobody ever hears it. The region field says UNSET. Why?</summary>

A node with no region set does not transmit. Region comes before every on-air step. <Cite ids="CUR-C01" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>2. A friend says "we're both on LongFast, so we're on the same channel". What else has to match for a channel message to be readable?</summary>

The region and frequency slot (same RF channel), and the logical channel's **key**. A preset is only the modem
settings; logical channel, key, slot and preset are separate settings. <Cite ids="MT-C15" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>3. Your app shows a check mark on a DM. Can you say the person read it?</summary>

No. At best it's an explicit ACK from their node, which is not a human acknowledgment and, in 2.7, not a signed
receipt. Only a reply tells you a person saw something. <Cite ids="MT-C14" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>4. Why is CLIENT the right first role, and not ROUTER?</summary>

`CLIENT` is an ordinary endpoint that can still relay. `ROUTER` changes relay timing and skips the ordinary
duplicate cancellation, which is meant for coordinated fixed sites; using it casually adds traffic for everyone
nearby. <Cite ids="MT-C05 HW-C08" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>5. You want to show someone your channel setup online. What can you share safely?</summary>

Region, preset, role and the fact that the channel uses a 32-byte key. Not the key, the channel URL or its QR code:
the URL encodes the key. <Cite ids="CUR-C12" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>Deeper dive: what the radio actually sends for this message</summary>

- Over the air, a Meshtastic frame starts with a 16-byte header in the clear: destination, sender, packet ID, a
  flags byte holding the hop limit and hop start, a **one-byte channel hash**, and next-hop and relay bytes.
  The channel's name never travels; the hash is only a hint for which key to try. <Cite ids="CUR-C04 MT-S20" />
- Channel keys are 16 bytes (AES-128) or 32 bytes (AES-256). One-byte shorthand keys pick values printed in the
  firmware source, including the default key, so they are effectively public. <Cite ids="CUR-C13 MT-C17" />
- With slot 0, the slot is `hash(primary channel name) % number of slots`; the number of slots depends on region
  and bandwidth. Explicit slots avoid surprises. <Cite ids="CUR-C03" />
- The hop limit counts relays, not RF links; on a two-node link it doesn't matter yet. Module 04 uses it. <Cite ids="MT-C08" />
- First contact with a community should be low-traffic: listen, then one short introduction, and coordinate
  repeated tests out of band. <Cite ids="NTX-C05" />
- No candidate device's FCC grant was confirmed by this course. Module 08 shows how to look one up. <Cite ids="REG-C06" />
</details>
