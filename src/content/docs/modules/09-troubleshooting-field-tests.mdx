When a message doesn't arrive, the temptation is to change five things at once. This module gives you a fixed
order, a decision tree for the common symptoms, and a log format that lets you prove what changed. The examples are
synthetic; the recovery steps come from manufacturer documents and were not run for this course.

## Goals and prerequisites

You need the first-link checkpoints from [Module 03](../03-first-meshtastic-link/) and
[Module 05](../05-first-meshcore-link/), and the PDR method from [Module 07](../07-reliability-capacity/). Without
hardware, use the synthetic logs in this lesson and the mismatch bench in the
[packet inspector](../../labs/packet-inspector/).

## The order: bottom layer first

1. **Power:** supply, cable, battery, charge temperature.
2. **USB or BLE:** does the computer see the device, does the phone pair?
3. **Firmware and role:** installed version and build target, role or image.
4. **Region:** set, and right for your hardware's band.
5. **RF parameters:** preset or frequency, bandwidth, spreading factor, coding rate, slot.
6. **Channel or contact:** same channel and key; for DMs, each side knows the other.
7. **Routing:** relays eligible, hop budget or path valid.
8. **Application delivery:** the recipient's app displays it; the ACK comes back.

</FlowDiagram>

| Step | Check | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Power | Supply and cable; battery polarity; charge temperature range | Screen or LED on; stable voltage |
| 2 USB / BLE | Data cable, other port, no other app holding the port; forget and re-pair BLE | Serial port or drive appears; app connects |
| 3 Firmware, role | Version and target read **from the device**; Meshtastic role setting; MeshCore image type <Cite ids="HW-C11 HW-C04" /> | Version string, board name |
| 4 Region | Region is set; a Meshtastic node with region unset doesn't transmit <Cite ids="CUR-C01" /> | Region field |
| 5 RF | Same preset or frequency, bandwidth, SF, CR and slot on both <Cite ids="MT-C15 CUR-C03" /> | Settings side by side |
| 6 Channel / contact | Same channel key; DM recipient's key known (Meshtastic) or mutual contacts (MeshCore) <Cite ids="MT-C19 MC-007" /> | Contact list, key fingerprint compared in person |
| 7 Routing | Relays eligible; hop budget; MeshCore stored path still valid <Cite ids="MT-C08 MC-005" /> | Hop fields; stored path |
| 8 App | Recipient displays it; ACK state on sender <Cite ids="MT-C13 MC-030" /> | Recipient screen; sender status |

Same protocol on both is a step-0 check: Meshtastic and MeshCore never exchange native messages. <Cite ids="LW-C02" />

## Symptom-to-test decision tree

Start at the symptom that matches. Each answer names the first test and why.

<details>
<summary>Local sends work, but multi-hop fails</summary>

Steps 1–6 are proven by the local success, so start at **routing**.

- **Meshtastic:** is the hop limit big enough for the number of relays? Is the middle node `CLIENT_MUTE` or in
  rebroadcast mode `NONE`? <Cite ids="MT-C08" />
- **MeshCore:** is the middle node a repeater, or a companion with repeating off? <Cite ids="MC-004" /> Is a stored
  path pointing at a relay that's gone? <Cite ids="MC-011" />
- **Test:** change one of these, send 10 numbered messages, count deliveries at the far end.
</details>

<details>
<summary>The link works one way only (asymmetric)</summary>

Measure both directions with the same count and spacing. Then check, one at a time:

- Transmit power or antenna differs between the two ends.
- One end is noisier (near electronics or another transmitter); compare SNR, not just RSSI. <Cite ids="RF-C12" />
- **MeshCore:** routes are learned per direction; D's path to A can be stale while A's to D is fine. Reset the stale
  one and re-test. <Cite ids="MC-012" />
- **Meshtastic:** an ACK may be lost on the way back while the message arrived. Check the recipient's screen. <Cite ids="MT-C13" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>A known node or contact never gets messages</summary>

"Known" proves you heard *something* once, not that a route works now.

- **Meshtastic:** the node appears in your list, but DMs fail: is its public key known (otherwise the DM refuses to
  send)? Is the saved next hop stale? The last retry floods; did that help? <Cite ids="MT-C19 MT-C11" />
- **MeshCore:** you heard an advert, but the contact has no path or a stale path. Reset the path and send again;
  a reset transmits nothing by itself. <Cite ids="MC-007 MC-012" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>Delivery works only when an IP bridge is on</summary>

That's not an RF link. The packet went over MQTT between two gateways. <Cite ids="MT-C22" />

- Check the via-MQTT flag on received packets (Meshtastic header). <Cite ids="CUR-C04" />
- Check which channels have uplink and downlink enabled. <Cite ids="CUR-C11" />
- **Test:** disable downlink (or the gateway) and repeat. If delivery drops to zero, the RF path doesn't exist yet.
  The [local MQTT appendix](../../appendix/local-mqtt/) walks through this diagnosis on synthetic data.
</details>

## Recovery decision tree

Recovery is for the device itself. **Every step marked ⚠ erases settings and identity keys.** Back up first, and
never use erase as a first diagnostic. <Cite ids="HW-C12" />

<details>
<summary>Powers on, but no serial port or drive appears</summary>

1. Known **data** cable, different port, no other app holding the port.
2. Enter the board's bootloader with its documented procedure (table below).
3. On RAK and T1000-E, a USB drive should appear; read its info file before doing anything else.
4. Reflash the exact image for your board. ⚠ Choosing "full erase" or "factory reset" erases.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Boots, but not in the role you expected</summary>

1. Read version and build target from the device.
2. Meshtastic: roles are a setting on one image; change the setting.
3. MeshCore: companion, repeater and room server are separate images; reflash the right one. ⚠ May erase depending
   on the procedure. <Cite ids="HW-C04" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>The phone won't pair after flashing</summary>

1. Forget the device in the phone's Bluetooth settings and pair again. <Cite ids="HW-S08" />
2. Check the app version against the baseline.
3. Try USB to separate a BLE problem from a firmware problem.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Two nodes still can't exchange anything</summary>

Go back to the eight-step order above, starting at step 3 on **both** nodes, and change one thing per test.
</details>

| Board | Bootloader entry | Erase warnings | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heltec WiFi LoRa 32 V3 | Hold PRG, tap RST, release PRG | Full-erase flashing options destroy settings and keys | <Cite ids="HW-S02 HW-C12" /> |
| RAK4631 | Double-tap reset for the UF2 drive; RAK4631-R needs its documented bootloader conversion first | A conversion or erase image wipes settings | <Cite ids="HW-S05 HW-C12" /> |
| Seeed T1000-E | Hold the button, connect the magnetic cable twice quickly | Use only the exact T1000-E image; **do not use NRF-OTA**; the erase image wipes everything | <Cite ids="HW-S07 HW-S08 HW-C06" /> |

## A test log that you can compare later

Use the same columns every time. The [field planner](../../labs/field-planner/) offers a blank CSV with these
columns and a synthetic example.

| Column | Kind | Example (synthetic) |
|---|---|---|
| Test ID, date and time | Record | T09-03, 2026-11-14 10:20 |
| Firmware, board, role | Record | 2.7.26 / RAK4631 / CLIENT |
| Modem settings | Record | Preset name, slot |
| Antenna and placement | Record | Stock antenna, vertical, window sill |
| Location label | Record | "Back porch" (never coordinates) |
| Obstruction | Record | One brick wall |
| Sent, received | **Measurement** | 20 scheduled, 17 displayed at D |
| RSSI, SNR | **Measurement** | As reported by the receiver |
| Latency | **Measurement** | Seconds from send to display |
| Route observation | **Inference** unless read from a packet | "Probably via B" |
| Notes | Either; label which | "Wall may matter (inference)" |

Rules for comparing runs: <Cite ids="CUR-C23" />

1. Keep RF settings, endpoints and firmware fixed.
2. Change **one** variable per repeat, and write which one.
3. Report PDR with its sample size. <Cite ids="CUR-C22" />
4. Mark each note as measurement or inference.
5. Keep the cadence gentle: a few messages a minute at most on a shared channel.

## Worked example: three runs, one conclusion

A synthetic log for a two-node Meshtastic link that "sometimes fails":

```text
SYNTHETIC LOG - not a measurement
T09-01  A→D  20 sched  12 shown  ACKs 11  RSSI −112..−118  SNR −9..−14  antennas vertical, D behind wall
T09-02  A→D  20 sched  12 shown  ACKs 12  same conditions, repeated next morning
T09-03  A→D  20 sched  19 shown  ACKs 18  ONE CHANGE: D's node moved 1 m to the window, same room
```

- Measurement: PDR 60 % (12/20), 60 % (12/20), 95 % (19/20).
- Inference: the wall or the position inside the room matters at this distance. Supported by one controlled change
  after two matching baselines. Not supported: "windows add 35 %", which would need many runs and other days.
- What the ACK column adds: ACKs track displays closely here, but in T09-03 one message was shown without an ACK,
  so ACKs undercount delivery by 1.

## Your turn: diagnose from evidence

A MeshCore group reports: "Alex can message Robin, but Robin's replies never reach Alex. Both see each other in
contacts. It started after the hilltop repeater was replaced last week." Which step of the order do you start
at, what's your first test, and what would confirm it?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Start at **routing** (step 7): steps 1–6 are shown to work by Alex→Robin succeeding. The replaced repeater has a new
identity and prefix, so Robin's stored path back to Alex likely still names the old repeater. <Cite ids="MC-011" />
First test: reset Robin's path to Alex, then Robin sends one message. <Cite ids="MC-012" /> Confirmation: it floods,
arrives, and later replies go direct on the new path. If it still fails, check the new repeater's radio settings
and forwarding flag (step 5 and eligibility). <Cite ids="MC-005" />
</details>

## Knowledge check

<details>
<summary>1. A friend's first move with any problem is "factory reset". Why is that a poor first diagnostic?</summary>

It erases settings and identity keys, so contacts see a new node, and it destroys the evidence of what was wrong.
It belongs at the end of the recovery tree, after a backup. <Cite ids="HW-C12" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>2. Your node hears a neighbor with RSSI −60 dBm, but DMs fail. Which steps can you skip?</summary>

Probably power and the RF link for that direction. Still check channel or contact, routing and the return path:
strong RSSI doesn't prove decoding, keys or routing. <Cite ids="RF-C12" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>3. Is "probably went via B" a measurement?</summary>

No, unless you read it from packet fields (relay byte, path). Label it an inference. <Cite ids="CUR-C23" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>4. Delivery works across town only when the gateway is on. What have you shown?</summary>

That the IP path works, not the RF path. Turn downlink off and repeat to test RF alone. <Cite ids="CUR-C11" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>Deeper dive: when "one variable" isn't possible</summary>

Some changes drag others along: moving a node changes distance, obstruction and noise at once; reflashing changes
firmware and erases keys. Then write down everything that changed, run more repeats, and narrow the conclusion to
the combination you actually tested.
</details>
