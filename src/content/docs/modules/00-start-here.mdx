## What just happened

You sent a message through four separate layers. Most confusion about mesh radios comes from mixing them
up, so here they are with one more term you will meet: LoRaWAN.

| Term | What it is | On the trace above |
|---|---|---|
| **Radio settings** | The frequency, bandwidth, spreading factor and coding rate a radio transmits and listens on. Matching them puts both radios on the same channel. To accept a whole frame, the receiver also needs compatible framing, such as the same sync word. <Cite ids="LW-C04" /> | Not shown; both radios agree. |
| **LoRa modulation** | The way the radio turns bits into a radio signal and back. It is a *physical layer*: it carries bits, not messages. <Cite ids="LW-C01" /> | The wavy hop from radio A to radio B. |
| **Mesh protocol** | The packet format and forwarding rules on top of LoRa: who sent it, who should relay it, how duplicates are dropped. Meshtastic and MeshCore each have their own. <Cite ids="LW-C02" /> | Radio A builds a packet; radio B opens it. |
| **Phone–radio link** | A short local link, often Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) or USB, carrying an *app protocol* between your phone and your own radio. It is not the over-the-air packet. <Cite ids="LW-C03 MC-018" /> | The dotted hops at each end. |
| **LoRaWAN** | A different system that also uses LoRa: devices send to gateways, which forward over the internet to a network server. It is not a peer-to-peer mesh, and this course does not use it. <Cite ids="LW-C01" /> | Not involved. |

## Worked example: same radio, different protocol

Two neighbors each buy the same model of radio. Rin installs Meshtastic, Dev installs MeshCore, and by
coincidence both radios are set to the same frequency, bandwidth, spreading factor and coding rate.
Rin sends "hello" from the Meshtastic app. There are three separate questions about what reaches Dev:

1. **Is there signal on Dev's channel?** Yes. Rin's transmission lands on the channel Dev's radio is tuned
   to, so Dev's radio can notice radio activity there.
2. **Does Dev's radio receive a complete frame?** Those four settings alone don't decide this. A LoRa
   receiver also checks framing details, such as the sync word, before it hands a frame to the firmware.
   The two projects set their sync word in different code: Meshtastic sets its own value, while MeshCore
   passes a named constant from the RadioLib radio library. This course hasn't tested what one stock radio does with the other's
   transmissions, so don't assume a frame arrives. <Cite ids="LW-C04" />
3. **Could Dev's app show the message?** Even in a *hypothetical* setup where every framing detail matched and
   Dev's radio passed up the frame's bytes, Dev's MeshCore firmware would try to read them as a MeshCore
   packet. The two packet formats differ, so nothing would appear in Dev's app. <Cite ids="LW-C02" />

Matching radio settings put the signal on the same channel. Receiving a frame also needs compatible framing,
and getting the message across needs the same mesh protocol.

<LayerStack />

A special application-level gateway that understood both formats would be a separate system. The core
labs don't assume one exists.

## Why it works without the internet

Every hop in the trace was local: phone to its own radio, then radio to radio over the air. No tower,
router or server took part. That gives you a quick way to work out what still works when services fail:

| What fails | Does the message still get through? |
|---|---|
| Internet is down | Yes, if the radios are in range with matching settings and protocol. |
| Cellular network is down | Yes, for the same reason. |
| Your phone's battery dies | You can't type or read, but your radio can still relay other people's packets if its role allows it. <Cite ids="MT-C07 MC-005" /> |
| The radios are out of range of each other, with no relay between | No. Some radio has to hear each transmission. |
| You turned on an internet bridge such as MQTT | That bridge stops working, but the radio path doesn't depend on it. Bridges are optional, separate data paths with their own privacy questions. <Cite ids="MT-C22" /> |

Off-grid mesh is a **supplementary** way to communicate. Delivery varies with terrain, antennas, settings and
traffic, and later lessons show how to measure it rather than assume it.

## Predict before you check

Without scrolling to the answers: Dev retunes the MeshCore radio to a frequency well outside the channel
Rin transmits on, so Rin's signal no longer falls within the band Dev's radio is listening to. Rin sends
"hello" again. Where does it fail now, and how is that different from the worked example?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

It now fails at the very bottom: Rin's signal isn't on Dev's channel, so there is nothing for Dev's radio to
receive. No frame forms and nothing reaches the protocol layer. In the worked example the signal *was* on
Dev's channel; what happened next depended on framing, and even with matching framing the protocol layer
would have rejected it. To Dev every case looks the same ("no message"). That is why later troubleshooting
lessons check the layers in order, from the bottom up.
</details>

## Knowledge check

<details>
<summary>1. Rin and Dev both run Meshtastic now, with matching settings. Rin's phone has no cell signal. Can Rin's message reach Dev?</summary>

Yes, if the radios are in range or connected by relays. The phone only needs its local BLE link to its own
radio, and the radios talk to each other directly over LoRa. Cell signal plays no part.
</details>

<details>
<summary>2. Is "LoRa" the same thing as "LoRaWAN"?</summary>

No. LoRa is the modulation, the physical layer. LoRaWAN is a network specification built on LoRa, with
gateways and a network server, usually reached over the internet. Meshtastic and MeshCore also use LoRa,
but neither is LoRaWAN. <Cite ids="LW-C01" />
</details>

<details>
<summary>3. A friend says: "My radio picked up a signal from a MeshCore node, so my Meshtastic app should show its messages." What is wrong?</summary>

Picking up a signal only shows there was energy on a shared channel. Receiving a whole frame also needs
compatible framing, such as the sync word, and showing a message needs the same mesh protocol, because
each system defines its own packet format. <Cite ids="LW-C04 LW-C02" />
</details>

## Choose your learning path

All three paths use the same lessons. The difference is what you do in the optional exercises.

- **No hardware.** Every required lesson and lab works in the browser with simulations and sample data. Start
  with [Module 01](../01-rf-foundations/) and try the [routing playground](../../labs/routing-playground/).
- **Two radios.** The same lessons plus optional exercises with two of your own nodes. Read
  [Module 02](../02-hardware-first-boot/) before buying or powering anything; it covers band, antenna and
  rule checks first.
- **Operator.** For people planning a small neighborhood network. Modules on reliability, security and rules,
  field tests and deployment are planned; the [course outline](../../curriculum/) shows what is available now.

<details>
<summary>Engineering detail: where the layers live in each project</summary>

- Meshtastic describes its packets with Protocol Buffers; the selected firmware pins a specific protobuf schema
  commit. <Cite ids="MT-S04" />
- MeshCore documents its over-the-air header, optional transport codes and path encoding separately from the
  *companion protocol* that its phone and computer apps use over BLE or USB. <Cite ids="MC-S08 MC-S15" />
- In both systems a node's *role* decides whether it relays for others, independently of whether a phone is
  connected. <Cite ids="MT-C07 MC-005" />
- How this course labels what it tells you: a **teaching model** is a deliberate simplification, like the trace
  above; a **design assumption** is an example value; a **documented or measured fact** cites a source with
  its version and date.
</details>
