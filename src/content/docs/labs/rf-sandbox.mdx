This lab turns two calculations from [Module 01](../../modules/01-rf-foundations/) into something you can
change and watch: how long one LoRa packet stays on the air, and how much signal is left after a chain of
gains and losses. Every input is a teaching value you choose. Nothing here measures a real radio.

## What the calculator covers, and what it doesn't

- **Airtime formula.** The Semtech SX1276/77/78/79 datasheet (Rev. 4, §4.1.1.7, p. 31): symbol time
  Ts = 2^SF / BW, preamble time (n + 4.25)·Ts, and payload symbols
  8 + max(ceil((8·PL − 4·SF + 28 + 16·CRC − 20·IH) / (4·(SF − 2·DE))) · (CR + 4), 0). <Cite ids="RF-C01" />
- **Reference subset.** SF7–SF12 at 125, 250 or 500 kHz. SF5, SF6 and narrower bandwidths such as 62.5 kHz
  are left out because this formula and benchmark set weren't cross-checked for them. That doesn't make them
  illegal or unsupported on every radio; other chips have their own references.
- **Low-data-rate optimization (DE).** The datasheet mandates it when a symbol lasts over 16 ms. The
  calculator switches it automatically when you change SF or bandwidth, and flags a mismatch if you override
  it. <Cite ids="RF-C02" />
- **Payload bytes** are the physical-layer bytes handed to the radio, including any mesh header or encryption
  bytes. They are not text characters, and the calculator doesn't add any protocol's overhead for you.
  <Cite ids="RF-C05" />
- **Rounded up** shows the packet time rounded up to a whole millisecond, the way Semtech's LoRaMac-node
  driver reports it; the exact value is shown alongside. <Cite ids="RF-C16" />
- **Aggregate airtime** is identical packets added end to end: transmit time only, with no gaps, channel
  waiting, ACKs, relays or retries unless you count them as extra packets.
- **Benchmarks.** The two airtime fixtures (56.576 ms and 1318.912 ms) were cross-checked against the
  tagged driver code; the two link-budget fixtures use synthetic values and an assumed sensitivity.
  <Cite ids="RF-C03 RF-C04 RF-C07" />

## Experiment 1: spreading factor and airtime

- **Setup.** Press *Reset to worked examples*: SF7, 125 kHz, CR 4/5, 20 bytes, 10 packets.
- **Change one thing.** Raise the spreading factor one step at a time from SF7 to SF12.
- **Expect.** The symbol time doubles at every step (1.024 ms at SF7, 32.768 ms at SF12). The packet time
  roughly doubles too. At some steps it's a little less (SF7 → SF8 is ×1.82) because fewer payload symbols
  are needed; at others it's exactly double (SF9 → SF10). At SF11 and SF12 low-data-rate optimization switches on. SF12 takes 1318.912 ms per packet, about 13.19 s for ten.
  <Cite ids="RF-C03 RF-C04 RF-C15" />
- **If you see something else.** If an error appears instead of a result, check the advanced settings: you
  may have overridden low-data-rate optimization. If the numbers differ from the fixtures, another input,
  such as the preamble, header or CRC, isn't at its worked-example value.

## Experiment 2: what the bytes cost

- **Setup.** Reset, then open the advanced settings.
- **Change one thing.** Switch the explicit header off (implicit header).
- **Expect.** Payload symbols drop from 43 to 38 and the packet from 56.576 ms to 51.456 ms. Turning CRC off
  instead gives the same 38 symbols here. The formula counts these as bit terms; the calculator doesn't
  invent byte positions for them. <Cite ids="RF-C01" />
- **If you see something else.** At other payload sizes the ceiling step can absorb a small change, so
  removing the header or CRC may not change the symbol count at all. That's the formula rounding to whole
  symbol blocks, not a bug.

## Experiment 3: an obstruction in the link budget

- **Setup.** Reset: 14 dBm, 2 dBi each end, 1 dB cable loss each end, 115 dB assumed path loss, −120 dBm
  assumed sensitivity.
- **Change one thing.** Tick *Add an assumed 25 dB obstruction*.
- **Expect.** Received power falls from −99 dBm to −124 dBm, and the margin from 21 dB to −4 dB.
  <Cite ids="RF-C07" />
- **If you see something else.** If the margin isn't 21 dB before the change, one of the defaults was
  edited. If you get a different sensitivity from a datasheet, remember it applies only at that chip's
  stated spreading factor, bandwidth and error criterion.

A negative margin in this model means the *assumed* received power is below the *assumed* sensitivity. It
doesn't predict whether a real message arrives, and no part of this lab decides what is legal to transmit.
